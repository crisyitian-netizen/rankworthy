# W6 Off-site authority: mentions, links, YouTube, communities

## Brand mentions matter most for AI visibility
- In Ahrefs' study of 75,000 brands, web mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility more strongly than backlinks or domain rating; YouTube mentions had the strongest correlation of the factors tested. Correlation, not proof of cause. `[Study]`
- Unlinked and nofollow mentions still count for AI: models learn which brands appear next to which topics.
- Must be real. Google lists chasing inauthentic mentions as ineffective. `[Google]`
- Keep the brand description (what, where, for whom) identical everywhere.

## Where to appear (build a table: source, listed?, action, owner)
1. Review and listing sites: G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, Product Hunt, AlternativeTo (software); Google Business Profile, Trustpilot, TripAdvisor, industry review sites, app stores (local and consumer).
2. Industry directories, associations and trade media.
3. List articles: "best X", "X for beginners", "X compared", "X for <use case>". Find ones that already rank or that AI cites, contact the author with something useful (new prices, features, an angle) and ask to be included or updated. Publish your own honest comparisons and "alternatives to X for <audience>" pages; peers can list each other. Works now, is easily abused, may not last; keep it truthful. `[Practice]`
4. Communities: Reddit, Quora, niche forums, LinkedIn articles, Medium.
5. Your own channels: YouTube, podcast, newsletter.

Use W7 results: the sources AI cites where you are absent are the shortlist.

## Reddit and communities
- Comment on existing high-upvote threads rather than posting new ones; AI cites whole threads including top comments.
- Monitor brand, competitor and category terms (e.g. F5Bot, free).
- Share real experience ("we switched to X because Y"), answer unanswered specific questions, acknowledge criticism and give a fix.
- No disguised ads, no copy-paste recommendations across threads, links only when useful, personal accounts with history, no AI-sounding text.

## Links
- Relevance and naturalness: 5 links from relevant, stronger sites beat 100 junk links.
- Mixed profile: dofollow and nofollow, mostly brand anchors, few exact-match anchors; do not chase only high DA.
- Avoid "news" sites that publish anything for a fee; Google mostly ignores those links.
- Check the real domain, not just DR (a subdomain on a high-DR host is not that authority). Insertions into old articles that already rank and get traffic often beat new posts.
- Prefer digital PR: data-led stories, interviews, events, original research.
- Paid links violate Google's link spam policy and carry risk. If asked, explain it; the owner decides.
- Prospecting searches: `<topic> resources`, `best <category> tools`, `<competitor> alternatives`, `<topic> statistics`, `<topic> guide`, `<topic> templates`, `<topic> for <audience>`; also competitors' referring domains. Find public contact routes (author page, contact page, guidelines, professional profile); record only what you actually found, with the source; never guess emails. Local: partner businesses, chambers of commerce, local media.

## Brand search
Create reasons to search the brand by name. Practitioner example: short videos without a link that tell viewers to search "<course name> <creator name>" on YouTube, turning social reach into branded search. Build an owned channel (email list, community) that no algorithm controls.

## YouTube
- YouTube is among the domains most cited in Google AI Overviews; small channels get cited too. `[Study]`
- Make search-intent evergreen videos (how-to, comparisons, explainers), not viral bets. Keyword in title and the first two lines of the description, chapters with timestamps, say the keyword out loud, accurate captions.
- Also: sponsorships and collaborations to be mentioned in others' videos.
- For non-video businesses, 3-5 short "how to choose / how to order" videos are a realistic start.
