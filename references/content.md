# W3 Content and conversion

Goal: publish-ready pages that rank in Google, get quoted by AI, and make the visitor act.

## Workflow
1. Business context (project profile) → 2. one target keyword/cluster and its intent (keywords.md) → 3. read the SERP and the AI Overview → 4. outline → 5. write with first-hand input → 6. title/meta → 7. internal links → 8. de-slop pass → 9. owner review → 10. publish and log the change.

If first-hand input is missing (data, cases, photos, tests), ask the owner for it. Do not invent it.

## Title and meta description
- Title = keyword + a hook for humans ("Top hats: maximum elegance", not "Buy top hats tall online"). ≤ ~60 characters. Google may rewrite it.
- Meta description: concrete reasons to click (price from, free shipping, 24-hour dispatch, warranty, minimum order). AI drafts are usually flat; add the spark by hand.

## Landing page structure
1. **Strategic H1**: contains the main keyword; may be small. The marketing headline can be separate.
2. **Value proposition in the first two lines**: what you do, for whom. No "best agency in town".
3. **H2/H3** cover secondary keywords and sub-topics (fan-out questions).
4. **Differentiators**: why you, stated plainly ("Comparing suppliers? Three real differences").
5. **FAQ** from keyword research and real customer questions, including buying objections (price, minimums, who it suits).
6. **Social proof**: reviews, cases, client logos, certifications.
7. **Clear CTA** (call, WhatsApp, quote, add to cart).

## Citability standard (check every section)
- **Answer first (BLUF)**: the first 30-60 words under each H2 answer the question. Both people and AI weight the start of a passage most. `[Study]`
- **Self-contained paragraphs**: name the subject; avoid "it/this" pointing back; do not open with "But/However". AI splits pages into chunks you do not control. (Google says chunking is not required for its AI features; this is a readability habit that also helps extraction.) `[Practice]`
- **Question-style headings** that match how people ask.
- **Named entities**: specific products, places, numbers. "Ahrefs Keywords Explorer finds low-difficulty keywords" beats "this tool helps with SEO".
- **One idea per sentence**, plain subject-verb-object.
- **Verifiable**: sources, expert quotes, stats with year. The 2024 GEO paper (KDD) found citations, quotations and statistics raised visibility in generative answers; keyword stuffing did not. `[Study]`
- **First-hand data**: own surveys, tests, numbered cases ("we changed the primary category on 5 listings; in two months they moved from #X to #Y").
- **Formats** that get cited: lists, comparison tables, steps. Length is not a target; short precise pages get cited. One analysis of 174k AI Overview citations found near-zero correlation between word count and citation `[Study]`.
- **Freshness**: update facts, prices, years when they change and show the update date. Only real updates.
- **Multimodal**: original images, charts, and video (see offsite.md).

## Answer-block templates
- **Definition** ("What is X"): H2 as question; one-sentence definition; 1-2 sentences of key traits; one sentence on why it matters.
- **Steps** ("How to X"): one-line overview; numbered steps "**Step name**: 1-2 sentences"; expected result or time.
- **Comparison** ("X vs Y"): table of criteria with a "Best for" row; 1-2 sentence verdict.
- **Pros and cons** ("Is X worth it"): ~3 each; balanced verdict.
- **FAQ**: one heading per question; 40-60 word answer first, then detail.

## Length floors (coverage, not targets)
Service page ~800 words, blog post ~1,500, product page 300+, location page 500-600. Word count is not a ranking factor; a short page that fully answers wins. Ignore "keyword density 1-2%" rules.

## Google's Who / How / Why test `[Google]`
Who wrote it (byline, credentials)? How was it made (first-hand experience, original research; disclose AI help where readers would care)? Why does it exist (to help people, not to catch clicks, hit a word count or fake freshness)? Weak on all three = at risk.

## De-slop pass
Remove common AI tells: strings of em dashes (use commas, colons, parentheses), "delve / leverage / utilize / foster / streamline / navigate the landscape" and their equivalents in other languages, formulaic triplets, empty summary sentences. Goal is natural, readable text, not hiding AI involvement. Never use this register in community posts.

## Priority rewrites
From Search Console: the 10 pages with the most impressions and fewest clicks, and quick-win queries at positions 8-20. Add one thing competitors cannot copy to each, fix title/meta, add internal links. Faster than new articles.

## UX and conversion (CRO)
- **5-second rule**: within 5 seconds a visitor knows what you do, who you are, why you, how to contact.
- Phone first: fast, no layout shift, readable, thumb-friendly CTAs.
- Fewer steps to the goal (order, booking, quote).
- Google uses behaviour signals (clicks, dwell; Navboost from the 2024 antitrust documents) `[Study]`. Perfect on-page SEO loses to a better experience.
- AI CRO review: capture a full-page screenshot and audit it against this list; the human decides.

## E-commerce product pages
Title = product + key feature + brand (≤60 chars); description with price or "from" and a CTA; one H1 = product name, unique across variants; H2s for features, specs, reviews, related; 3+ images (hero, detail, in use) with descriptive file names and alt; breadcrumbs Home > Category > Sub > Product; related products; Product schema with price (if public), availability, reviews.

## Programmatic pages (service × city, directories, glossaries)
Each page must stand alone: at least 30-40% genuinely different content between any two pages, not just swapped names; skip records without enough data; hub → spoke links; review a 5-10% sample before publishing; above ~100 pages, do a content review first. Google's scaled content abuse policy targets exactly this pattern. `[Google]`

## Content operations
Research brief (keyword, top-10 review, gaps) → write → optimize → de-slop → publish → review performance after 4-8 weeks. Repurpose a long article into social posts, an email, a video script and community answers. Scheduled auto-publishing still needs unique input and human review per piece.
